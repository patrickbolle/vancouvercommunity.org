There's a special kind of friendship that starts over a workbench. Someone sees you struggling with a jig, wanders over, and says, "Here, try it this way." Ten minutes later you're both covered in sawdust and talking about the next project.

That's what a makerspace gives you: tools you couldn't fit in an apartment, and people who love to make things. Vancouver has a good mix, from a volunteer-run hackerspace to a huge shared workshop to free repair nights at the library. Here's how to pick one. Every place below comes from [the directory's maker spaces page](/maker-spaces/).

## If you want a hackerspace

**[Vancouver Hack Space (VHS)](/maker-spaces/#vancouver-hack-space-(vhs))** is a volunteer-run, not-for-profit workshop at 1601 Venables St in East Van. Members share 3D printers, laser cutters, woodworking tools, robotics, welding and more. Their own motto is to "share tools, equipment, space, knowledge, and good times." I love that "good times" made the list.

The best part for newcomers: [VHS says](https://vanhack.ca/wp/) it's open to the public every Tuesday evening. You can drop by and look around, or book a tour for another time.

And if you want a reason to keep coming back, **[Vancouver Battlebots](/maker-spaces/#vancouver-battlebots)** fights battlebots at the Hack Space, using NHRL rules. Building a robot with friends is a fine way to spend a winter.

## If you want a big, well-equipped shop

**[MakerLabs](/maker-spaces/#makerlabs)** has 26,000 square feet of workshops at 780 East Cordova Street. There's a wood shop, metal shop, ceramics, CNC, laser cutters and sewing. According to [MakerLabs](https://www.makerlabs.com/), membership is month-to-month, and they run classes and workshops too. A class is a gentle way in. You learn a tool and meet the people beside you at the same time.

## If you want a collective

**[Vancouver Community Laboratory (CoLab)](/maker-spaces/#vancouver-community-laboratory-(colab))** is a collaborative workshop for wood, metal and textiles, at 1907 Triumph St. [The CoLab](https://vancommunitylab.com/) calls itself a member-based collective for makers, craftspeople, artists and tinkerers. You go to a monthly meeting before you join the waitlist, so you'll meet people before you ever pick up a tool.

## If you just need a tool or a fix

You don't need a membership to start making.

**[Vancouver Tool Library](/maker-spaces/#vancouver-tool-library)** lets you borrow tools instead of buying them. It's a cooperative at 3448 Commercial Street, with tools for home repair, gardening and bike maintenance. It also runs affordable public workshops on tool skills.

**[Repair Cafés Vancouver](/maker-spaces/#repair-caf%C3%A9s-vancouver)** are free community repair events. [SPEC](https://spec.bc.ca/waste/repair-cafe/) runs them with skilled volunteer fixers. Bring small appliances, electronics, clothing, bikes, laptops and more. Walk-ins are welcome, but book an appointment if you want to be sure there's time for your item. Watch a fixer work and you'll learn a skill too.

## Other places to make things

- **[Maker Cube (Langley)](/maker-spaces/#maker-cube-(langley))** covers everything from woodworking to 3D printing to pottery. Worth the drive if you live south of the Fraser.
- **[Emily Carr University Digital Fabrication Lab](/maker-spaces/#emily-carr-university-digital-fabrication-lab)** gives access to fabrication tools for students and community. It leans creative and art-focused.

## How to fit in at a makerspace

New makers often worry they'll look clumsy. Everyone in the room was a beginner once, and most of them remember it.

- **Go to the open night or a class first.** It's the easiest way to meet people.
- **Bring a small project.** "I'm trying to build a shelf" starts more conversations than "I'm just looking."
- **Ask for help.** Makers love to show off a technique. Let them.

Then go back. The third visit is when people start asking about your project. That rule is the core of my guide to [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).
