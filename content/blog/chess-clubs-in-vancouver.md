Chess is a good way to meet people for one simple reason. You sit across from a stranger for half an hour, and nobody expects small talk. The game does the talking. By the second game you know their name. By the third week you know their favourite opening and how they take their coffee.

Vancouver has more chess than you'd guess. Some of it happens in cafés, some at a university, some at rated club nights where people track every point. Here's where to go, sorted by the kind of player you are. Every group below comes from [the directory's chess page](/chess/).

## If you just want a casual game

Café chess is the easiest start. No membership, no rating, no sign-up form. You bring yourself, and maybe a board.

- **[Coco Rico Cafe Chess](/chess/#coco-rico-cafe-chess)** has informal chess on Friday and Saturday nights at 1290 Robson Street. The advice from the listing is to just show up. I like that.
- **[Main Street Café Chess](/chess/#main-street-caf%C3%A9-chess)** meets on Saturdays at Bean Around the World, at Main and 10th. It's casual. One report counted 18 players squeezed into the café, so go early if you want a seat.

If you've been searching for a chess café in Vancouver, these are the two to try first.

## If you want chess with a social side

**[Vancouver Beer & Chess](/chess/#vancouver-beer-%26-chess)** pairs chess with drinks. Their own description is my favourite pitch in the whole directory: "We play chess and get to know people who like chess." That's it. That's the club. If you moved here and want new friends more than a better rating, start here. They post on Meetup and Instagram.

## If you want to get better

Playing the same people every week is how you improve. It's also how you make friends, which is a nice side effect.

- **[UBC Chess Club](/chess/#ubc-chess-club)** is open to students and the wider community. It mixes casual and competitive play. They meet Mondays from 5pm to 8pm in LIFE2212.
- **[North Shore Chess Club](/chess/#north-shore-chess-club)** holds weekly meetups on the North Shore and welcomes all skill levels. A good pick if you live over the bridge.
- **[Vancouver Chess School](/chess/#vancouver-chess-school)** runs chess classes and community programs for youth and adults. Useful if you want real structure, or if your kid has started beating you.

## If you want rated games and tournaments

- **[Langley Chess Club](/chess/#langley-chess-club)** plays CFC rated games most Monday nights and runs blitz tournaments. It's a mix of competitive and casual players, so you won't feel out of place on your first visit. It is a drive from Vancouver, so plan for it.
- **[Vancouver Chess School](/chess/#vancouver-chess-school)** also runs tournaments, on top of its classes.
- **[BC Chess Federation Clubs](/chess/#bc-chess-federation-clubs)** is a directory of formal chess clubs. Use it to find a rated club near you if none of the above fits your week.

## What about park chess?

You might be hoping for a game in the park. I don't have a regular park group listed yet, and I won't send you to one I can't confirm. If you know a spot with a standing game, please tell me and I'll add it.

## How to stick with it

Chess clubs reward the same rule as every other group in this city: go three times before you decide. The first night you're the new face. The second night someone remembers your last game. The third night you're a regular, and someone asks you for a rematch. That rule is the core of my guide to [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).

One more tip: lose gracefully and ask questions. Most chess players love to explain what you did wrong. Let them. It's the fastest way into a conversation.
