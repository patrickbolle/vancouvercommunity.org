Karaoke is a strange gift. Almost nobody is good at it, and that's the point. You stand up, you get it a bit wrong, and a room of strangers cheers anyway. Not many places in a city let you be bad at something in public and still feel welcome.

Vancouver has a few kinds of karaoke. There are private rooms where only your friends can hear you. There are bars with a weekly night and a crowd. And there are quieter daytime sessions for people who'd rather not sing at midnight. Here's how to pick, sorted by how brave you feel. Every spot below comes from [the directory's karaoke page](/karaoke/).

## Private karaoke rooms

If you want to sing without an audience of strangers, book a room. You get your own space, your own queue and nobody else's birthday party.

**[SWITCH Karaoke](/karaoke/#switch-karaoke)** has private karaoke suites at 1339 Robson Street. It's open daily, and group rates are available, so it works well for a birthday or a team night out.

**[KTV Party World (Burnaby)](/karaoke/#ktv-party-world-(burnaby))** has private karaoke rooms and a large song selection. It's at 200-4361 Kingsway in Burnaby. Expect a vibrant, fun night out. If your song is obscure, a big catalogue matters more than you'd think.

## Karaoke bars and weekly nights

A private room is safe. A bar is where the magic happens. Someone you've never met belts out a power ballad, and suddenly the whole room is singing along.

**[Celebrities Nightclub Sunday Karaoke](/karaoke/#celebrities-nightclub-sunday-karaoke)** runs every Sunday at Vancouver's oldest gay bar, at 1022 Davie St. It's fun and inclusive, with a great atmosphere. Sunday is also a kind slot for a first try. Nobody expects much from a Sunday.

**[Union Latinos Karaoke Thursdays](/karaoke/#union-latinos-karaoke-thursdays)** brings a Latin vibe to karaoke night. The first 10 singers win a free Colombian hot dog. I can't think of a better reason to put your name down early.

Karaoke nights come and go faster than most events. **[Karaoke Schedule in Metro Vancouver (Facebook)](/karaoke/#karaoke-schedule-in-metro-vancouver-(facebook))** is a community group that tracks them. Its listings stay up to date across the city, so check it before you head out.

## If you're shy about singing

Plenty of people love karaoke and dread the microphone. That's fine. You have options.

Start in a private room with two or three friends. Once you've sung in front of people you know, singing in front of strangers feels much less dramatic.

Or try **[Community Centre Lunchtime Karaoke](/karaoke/#community-centre-lunchtime-karaoke)**. Some community centres run casual daytime karaoke. It's friendly to an older crowd, and the mood is relaxed. Lunchtime takes away a lot of the pressure. Search the city's recreation listings to find a session near you.

## Karaoke as a way to meet people

Karaoke is secretly a great way to make friends. Everyone in the room has already agreed to look a bit silly, so the usual walls come down fast. You clap for a stranger's song, they clap for yours, and now you have something to talk about.

The trick is the same as with any group: go back. A weekly night like Sundays at Celebrities or Thursdays at Union Latinos works best for this. The first time, you're a stranger with a song slip. By the third, the regulars know your song. If you want more ideas, read my guide on [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).
