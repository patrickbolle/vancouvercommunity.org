Some people want small talk. Some people want to ask, "But what is a good life, really?" on a Tuesday night with strangers. If you're the second kind, Vancouver has a home for you.

Philosophy groups are a quiet gift for making friends. You skip the "so what do you do?" part and go straight to what people actually think. After one evening of arguing gently about free will, you know someone better than after a month of office lunches.

Here's where to go, sorted by the kind of conversation you want. Every group below comes from [the directory's philosophy page](/philosophy-intellectual/).

## If you're new and a bit nervous

**[Shy Philosophers](/philosophy-intellectual/#shy-philosophers)** is made for you. The name says it all. Their Meetup page calls it a welcoming discussion group for thoughtful people who enjoy big ideas in a safe setting. Each session centres on one philosophical topic, with a structured, open-ended discussion. No background in philosophy is required, just curiosity.

Some of their meetups have been at Spartacus Books on Commercial Drive. Check Meetup for the next one.

**[Socrates Café Vancouver](/philosophy-intellectual/#socrates-caf%C3%A9-vancouver)** is open philosophical inquiry for everyone, using the Socratic method. No expertise is required. Its listing points to Meetup's Socrates Café topic page, so search there for a Vancouver event.

## If you want a weekly habit

**[Practical Philosophy Club](/philosophy-intellectual/#practical-philosophy-club)** holds weekly in-person meetups. No expertise is required. The focus is on ideas you can use in everyday life. It has chapters around the world, and its Vancouver Meetup has held events downtown and in Coquitlam.

A weekly group is the easiest place to become a regular. Show up three weeks in a row and people will start saving you a seat.

## If you'd rather start from a book

**[Mind Matters: A Book Club for Big Ideas](/philosophy-intellectual/#mind-matters%3A-a-book-club-for-big-ideas)** reads books about big ideas: philosophy, sociology, science and more. Their Meetup page says they aim for two non-fiction books a month. Audiobooks are available through the Vancouver Public Library, which makes it easy to keep up. Some meetups have been at VPL's Central Branch.

A book gives everyone the same starting point. That helps a lot if you're shy. You can always say, "I loved chapter three," and the conversation takes off from there.

## If you want philosophy plus a social life

**[Vancouver Curiosity Club](/philosophy-intellectual/#vancouver-curiosity-club)** calls itself "a social calendar with a brain." It runs philosophy and essay nights (Plato, Shelley, Emerson, Bacon, framed around modern problems), plus meditation and journaling circles, silent reading parties, gallery visits, film discussions and restaurant outings. The listing describes it as intelligent but not academic, social but not chaotic. I think that's a lovely balance.

The directory also lists **[Vancouver Discussions Meetup](/philosophy-intellectual/#vancouver-discussions-meetup)** for general discussions on varied topics. When I checked in November 2026, its Meetup page showed the Curiosity Club, so the two now seem to be the same group.

## How to enjoy your first discussion

A few small tips for your first night:

- **Listen more than you talk the first time.** You'll learn the group's style, and people notice a good listener.
- **Ask questions.** The best discussions feel like a shared puzzle that everyone is working on together.
- **Stay for the chat after.** The real friendships start on the walk to the bus.

And go back. The third time is when a group starts to feel like yours. That rule is the core of my guide to [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).
