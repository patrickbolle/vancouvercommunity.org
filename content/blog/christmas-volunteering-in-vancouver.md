Every December, a lot of us get the same feeling. We want to help. We want to do something with our hands that isn't shopping. And we'd like some company while we do it.

Volunteering gives you all three. Here's where to start in Vancouver this holiday season, and how to sign up in time. Every group below comes from [the directory's volunteer page](/volunteer/). I checked each organization's own site in November 2026 for the details.

## Sign up early

This is the one tip that matters most. Most places need you to apply, attend an orientation or pass a check before your first shift. That takes time. December is also when everyone else wants to help.

So apply now, even if you can only give a few hours in the new year. Holiday help is lovely. January help is just as needed.

## Food banks

**[Vancouver Food Bank](/volunteer/#vancouver-food-bank)** is the first place most people think of. Volunteers sort food and help with distributions. It's direct, hands-on work.

Here's the honest bit. The Greater Vancouver Food Bank's portal for individual volunteers is closed right now while it onboards new people. It says it will accept new individual applications again starting in January 2027.

Groups are a different story. Community, corporate and school groups can apply now. A group needs at least 4 volunteers. So gather a few friends, coworkers or neighbours and apply together. You could do worse for a holiday outing.

A few things to know before you go:

- The minimum age is 15. Some roles have different age limits.
- Everyone signs up for shifts through the food bank's volunteer portal, My Impact.
- The food bank can't take drop-in volunteers, so don't just turn up.
- Individual volunteers attend an orientation and complete a criminal record check through the food bank. Corporate volunteers don't need the check.

## Winter evenings in Stanley Park

**[Stanley Park Ecology Society](/volunteer/#stanley-park-ecology-society)** does conservation work in Stanley Park all year. This winter, it also needs people for something cozier.

It's looking for volunteers to sell fresh popcorn at the Cob House beside the Miniature Train Plaza during Astra Lumina at Stanley Park. The event runs Wednesday to Sunday, from October 22 into December 2026. Typical shifts are 5:30 to 9:30pm, and the society says it can sometimes make shifts shorter or longer. You need to be 16 or older. The money raised supports its environmental education and conservation programs.

A warm stand, a cold night, and a steady line of happy people. That's a good shift.

If you'd rather get muddy, its **EcoStewards** remove invasive plants on the second and fourth Saturday of every month, 10am to 1pm. Sessions fill quickly, so register early. If weekdays suit you better, look at **DIRT**, the society's invasive plant team. It meets Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 10am to 1pm.

## Animals

**[SPCA Volunteer](/volunteer/#spca-volunteer)** is for animal lovers. The BC SPCA's roles include dog walking, cat wellness, small animal care and adoption counselling. You pick a location, fill in a short online application, then finish its training before your first shift.

It's a good one for families. Youth aged 16 to 18 can volunteer in certain roles with a parent's or guardian's consent. Youth aged 12 to 15 can help in certain roles with a parent or guardian beside them.

## Hands-on and outdoors

**[Habitat for Humanity Vancouver](/volunteer/#habitat-for-humanity-vancouver)** builds homes and runs ReStores. It's hands-on work that makes a difference.

**[Shoreline Rescue Club](/volunteer/#shoreline-rescue-club)** runs beach cleanups in Vancouver and nearby. They usually happen once a month, more in summer. Check their Instagram for the next date. A winter beach cleanup is a fine way to clear your head between holiday dinners.

## If you're not sure where to start

Browse a list first. **[Volunteer Vancouver](/volunteer/#volunteer-vancouver)** connects you with local nonprofits. You can search its opportunities by interest, from animal care to children and youth.

The **[City of Vancouver](/volunteer/#city-of-vancouver-volunteering)** has its own programs too, including stewardship, emergency support and community policing.

## Why it's a good way to meet people

Volunteering is one of the easiest ways to make friends in a new city. You have a job to do, so you don't have to think of small talk. You just pass the boxes, or the popcorn, and the conversation starts on its own.

The trick is to go back. One holiday shift feels good. A regular shift turns into a crew. If you want more ideas, read my guide on [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).
