There's something about listening to a record with other people. Nobody skips a track. Nobody checks their phone between songs. You hear an album the way the band meant it, start to finish, and then someone turns to you and says, "That second side, though."

Vancouver has a small, lovely vinyl scene. There's a proper listening bar, a pop-up that travels, a record shop that throws parties, and a handful of stores where you can lose an afternoon. Here's where to go, sorted by the kind of night you want. Every spot below comes from [the directory's vinyl page](/vinyl-listening-bars/).

## If you want a listening bar

**[Lala (at June Restaurant)](/vinyl-listening-bars/#lala-(at-june-restaurant))** is a 40-seat vinyl listening bar in the basement of June, at 3305 Cambie St. The listing sums it up as a "living room listening to records," and that's exactly the feeling. It's relaxed and curated.

When it opened, [Scout Magazine](https://scoutmagazine.ca/june-introduces-lala-a-new-vinyl-listening-bar/) reported that rotating DJs spin vinyl seven nights a week. It also said there are a few reservations on Resy, and walk-ins are always welcome. Things change, so check June's Instagram before you go.

If you've been searching for a vinyl bar in Vancouver, this is the one to try first.

## If you want a pop-up

**[Doze Hifi](/vinyl-listening-bars/#doze-hifi)** is a travelling vinyl pop-up with rotating vintage speakers. Saturdays lean disco and house. Sundays are jazz, soul and funk. Because it moves around, Instagram is the place to find the next one.

I like a pop-up for a first visit. Everyone there made a small effort to find it, so you already have something in common.

## If you want a listening party

**[Neptoon Records Listening Parties](/vinyl-listening-bars/#neptoon-records-listening-parties)** are album release parties at a well-loved record store. A new album comes out, and people gather to hear it together. Their website lists upcoming parties, and some include an artist signing.

This is the easiest way to meet people who love the same band you do. You don't need an opener. "Have you heard the new one yet?" works every time.

## Record shops worth a visit

Record shops aren't bars, but they are where vinyl people gather. Go on a Saturday, flip through the bins, and you'll end up in a chat about a pressing you've never heard of.

- **[Zulu Records](/vinyl-listening-bars/#zulu-records)** is a Vancouver legend at 1972 W 4th Ave in Kitsilano. It has listening stations, a huge selection and an in-house turntable refurbisher. Handy if your old deck needs some love.
- **[Red Cat Records](/vinyl-listening-bars/#red-cat-records)** is an independent record store at 4386 Main St.
- **[Audiopile](/vinyl-listening-bars/#audiopile)** sells new and used vinyl on Commercial Drive.
- **[Beat Street Records](/vinyl-listening-bars/#beat-street-records)** is a DJ-focused shop at 439 W Hastings St, with electronic, hip-hop and house.

## How to turn records into friends

Vinyl can feel like a solo hobby. You, your headphones, your shelf. But it doesn't have to be.

Pick one spot and become a regular. Go to the same pop-up twice in a month. Go to the next listening party at the same shop. Ask the person beside you what they're digging for. Record people love to talk about records, and the second conversation is always easier than the first. That rule is the core of my guide to [making friends in Vancouver](/blog/how-to-make-friends-in-vancouver/).
